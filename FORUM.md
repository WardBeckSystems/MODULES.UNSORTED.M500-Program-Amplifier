## Ward Beck m490c...and I'm brand new. Hi!

_WBS RACKMOUNTING · Jul 31, 2012|45_

> Hello PB-Kid.
>
> Well the radio modules (M490 series) have the same basic pre-amplifyer as the M470, M480, and M500 series. A couple of slight tweaks here and there but essentially the same (same transformer as well). As Phil supplied you with the link to the schematic - start here. Learn the routing, the pin configurations, where to place the EQ, etc... A good modification would be is to replace the trim pot that sets the in-put gain of the microphone with a long-shaft pot. This way you can attenuate both the in-put and the put-put of the module - a little more conventional, and gives you a few options for colour. That would be R25 - the sensitivity. We set them at the factory, and often the radio station would calibrate them to their needs as transmission lines/methods would change of the years. A 10k pot should be sufficient, but watch the taper as if you use the wrong one all the attenuation will be at one end!!!
>
> AS per Phil's schematic link - drop the EQ at the insert point (P2 6 and P2 F for the send and the return P2 7 and P2 H).
>
> And of course don't forget to PAyPal a little donation when the project is complete to the society - to keep it going for others to use like you!!!!
>
> Thank you for your interest in the society and Ward Beck!!!!

## Sonic difference between the M441's and the M480's

_WBS INFO · Nov 9, 2012|45_

> Good afternoon Andrew in Montreal!
>
> Phil will assist you with the EQ's for the M460 series and the M402, or I can if you visited Toronto. Long story they are all very different. The first 124 modules had a large input transformer, custom designed by dad of course. That was a card that evolved in to a modular format very quickly. Same innards, different mounting. No sonic difference. They are the most broad in frequency response and resolution. Next are the M600's - of which I use regularly - they are a completely different beast and will not be discussed, but are the next in the chain of evolution. The M460 units of which I use regularly, have a significantly smaller transformer (custom, Dad, etc..), and I feel are the absolute unit for any mixing (they add a little weight to the tone / LF resolution). Tracking they are great as well but I prefer the M600's for that. Next are the M470, M480, M490, M500 series. They all have a smaller transformer 9custom, Dad, etc..). This was designed to meed the changing marketplace - price point. The pre-amplifyer is a little different as well. They all have a sound. They are all exceptional units. They will indeed out-perform British and American Ward Beck (I like ya kid!), but are a flavour. How the low frequencies are cradled is a great place to start the debate. No frequencies are hyped or exaggerated, nor will you measure any ringing on the units - thus no compensating capacitors are really required. I have done several records to tape on my old T1202A (M480) and they are super celan, give you what you give them (like B&K / Schopes microphones), the M460 series gives a little flavour as the low frequencies are snug, but allowed to bloom (think M49), then the M124 cards (real early stuff) well you know how when you wake up in the morning, you cant really see for like 2 - 3 seconds? But at mid day you can read your neighbours e-mails from across the room ..... well these units are the mid-day eyes. Pure transistor softness, no harshness, all frequencies are in-focus, available, and useable. 
>
> The EQ's are very, VERY different. The M402 series are step attenuators, where the later series are all op-amp based. They are musical, easily fit in the mix, solve an issue, bring a guitar out just a little bit, have the 80's snare BARK!!! The M402's if you get the recording or the film ones you are fine, if you get a broadcast one (more common) I advise changing the HF point from 6k to 12k, or put a switch outside the module to change the HF at the flip of a switch. I use mine for gtr and vox most times when EQ is needed. The EQ's found in the M460 modules (and the rest of them for that matter) are all the same concept as each other. They are consistently variable, some are full parametric, some are semi, etc... They are very clean do not add weight to the tone (the module does that nicely!). Using them independently of a trasnformered module you will not experience any tone degradation or addition. they do not really impart a sonic signature. So it will not conflict with your signal, just knob twiddling will!!
>
> As mentioned the M600/M500 units are very diffident. They are amplifyers that are transformer coupled on the input and the output with a mid-stage trim. Hit them hard. You will want many, many more!!
>
> Hope this helps. But the best is to bug someone with a couple of modules, get a CD with which you are intimately familiar, and listen to the programme through the modules. 
>
> Regards,
>
> Wb!

> Hello Mark.
>
> Well you are comparing units that are over a decade in manufacture and design to each other with very different architecture and paradigm for both usage and manufacture. The earlier equipment one could argue that the LF has a little more "Bloom" to it. As the transformers were designed with no expense spared or considered in the earlier days. As time went on the M470, M480, M490, and M500 series were all the same transformer (custom designed to my fathers wishes of course) and the pre-amplifyer circuit was basically the same. The earlier M124 card and even the M600 series were a "I want is at any cost" from the broadcast and film industries which at the time dictated the standard of equipment and of recordings. So sonically your palate of colours will be very different from generation of module to generation, and they can compliment each other very nicely (Tony swears by the little can transformers for percussion and guitar). As for which is the best, which are most desireable - well the market certainly dictated which was wanted, used, and ultimately purchased in the 1000's.
>
